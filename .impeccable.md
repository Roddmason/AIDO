## Design Context

### Users
Local Control Center is used by a small team of technical operators running a Windows-native autonomous development control plane. They need to inspect jobs, approval gates, worker leases, recovery, memory, retrieval, agents, sandbox posture, pipelines, sessions, workspaces and integrations without losing trust in what the system is doing.

### Brand Personality
Editorial premium, precise, accountable. The interface should feel like an operations ledger from a serious engineering desk: calm enough for repeated use, distinctive enough to avoid the generic dark admin dashboard pattern, and explicit about risk, state, traceability and provenance.

### Aesthetic Direction
The product uses an editorial control-room language: print-inspired hierarchy, dense operational tables, disciplined surfaces, local Windows fonts, graphite ink, parchment/editorial neutrals and rare moss, amber and oxblood accents. It must not look like a dark cyan terminal, liquid glass shell, landing page, or decorative AI dashboard.

### Design Principles
- Lead with operational truth: show state, ownership, leases, approvals, audit and recovery before decoration.
- Use editorial hierarchy for comprehension, not marketing: serif moments only where they help orient the operator.
- Keep motion semantic: animate section changes and state transitions only when they represent actual system changes.
- Preserve locality and privacy: no external font/CDN dependencies; use Windows-native font stacks.
- Make risk visible: dangerous actions require explicit granular approval and clear policy context.
